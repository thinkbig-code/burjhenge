The sun
on the spire

Every day, from somewhere in Dubai, the setting or rising sun sits exactly on the tip of Burj Khalifa. Burjhenge tells you where to stand, at what minute, and how to get there.

01How it works

From any spot, the tip of the tower sits at a fixed height above your horizon. When the sun reaches that height in the direction of the tower, it sits on the spire.

The spot moves every day, because where the sun sets slides north along the horizon until June and back south until December. A fixed place, like your balcony, lies in its path only twice a year, once each way.

So each day it is really a line, not one spot. Stand anywhere along it, nearer the tower or farther out, and the sun still lands on the tip, only at a different minute. The main screen shows the point about 8 km out, where the tower sits nicely in a phone photo. For any other point on the line, a viewpoint, or your own balcony, use MY PLACE.

Closer to the tower the tip is higher, so the moment comes earlier. Farther away it comes just before sunset.

02Find your spot

Today. The main screen opens on today's spot: the date, the gold time when the sun touches the tip, and how far the spot is from Burj Khalifa. Arrows or the date change the day. For the next few days you also get the sky forecast for that minute next to the time: CLEAR, HAZY, DUSTY or CLOUDY. When the day's line runs past one of the viewpoints, the spot moves there, so you get a clear view of the tower: the distance then says BY and the viewpoint's name. The date picker marks those days with a gold dot.

Your own place. Open MY PLACE, allow your location or paste coordinates (long-press the place in Google Maps to copy them), and set your floor. Burjhenge finds the dates when the sun lands on the tip as seen from there. No place in mind? Pick one of the public viewpoints in the list, like Creek Harbour or Al Jaddaf Walk for sunset, or Jumeirah Night Beach for sunrise.

Everything is worked out on your phone. Your location is not sent anywhere. The forecast comes from Open-Meteo for the city as a whole.

04Take the shot

The point is not the pin on a map. It is standing there when the sun touches the tip.

SUNSETON THE SPIRE8 KM EASTLOOKING WEST
  • Be there 15 minutes early. The sun takes about two minutes to cross the tip. The centred moment lasts seconds.
  • Stay on the pin. Walking toward or away from the tower shifts the time by seconds. Stepping sideways moves the sun off the tip.
  • Watch the haze. Dust often hides the sun low on the horizon. A closer spot means a higher sun and better odds.
  • Expose for the sun and the tower turns into a clean silhouette.

06Questions

How accurate are the times?
The sun position matches NOAA's solar calculator to within a few seconds, with refraction included. The curve of the Earth is ignored, which shifts the moment by a few seconds at most. Times are in Dubai time.
What about sunrise?
Same idea, from spots west of the tower: Jumeirah, the coast, the sea. Tap SUNSET to switch to SUNRISE.
What if a building is in the way?
Then move along the line. The sun lands on the tip from anywhere on that day's line, so a few hundred metres nearer or farther often clears the view. Only the time shifts a little. Or pick a viewpoint in MY PLACE: those have a clear view of the tower.
Does it work from my balcony?
Yes, if it faces the tower from the east (for sunset) or the west (for sunrise). Open MY PLACE and set your floor.
Found a mistake or have an idea?
Write to us. If a time or a spot was off, say where you stood and what you saw.
