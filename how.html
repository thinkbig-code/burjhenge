The sun on the spire

Twice a year, from the right spot in Dubai, the setting or rising sun sits exactly on the tip of Burj Khalifa. Burjhenge tells you where to stand, at what minute, and how to get there.

How it works

From any spot, the tip of the tower is at a fixed height above your horizon: about 6° from 8 km away, about 15° from 3 km. The sun crosses that height once on the way down every evening, but only on a few days is it also in the direction of the tower at that moment. Burjhenge works out that geometry minute by minute for every day of the year, then picks a spot you can actually reach.

The closer you stand, the higher the tower looks and the earlier the moment comes. Farther away, the sun is lower and the moment comes just before sunset.

How to use it

  1. The date and the gold time are the moment to be there. Arrows or tap the date to change the day.
  2. ROUTE opens the spot in Google Maps. The car gives a spot on land, the boat a spot on the water, when there is one that day.
  3. Drag the timeline or press play to watch the sun move past the tip. It always glides back to the moment.
  4. MY PLACE finds the dates for your own balcony, office or favourite café. Set the floor for a better answer.

Tips for the photo

  • Be there 15 minutes early. The sun takes about two minutes to slide across the tip. The centred moment lasts a few seconds.
  • Small moves change the time, not the shot. Walking 50 m along the line toward or away from the tower shifts the moment by seconds. Stepping sideways moves the sun off the tip, so stay close to the pin.
  • Haze is the real risk. Dubai's dust often hides the sun in the last few degrees before it sets. A closer spot means a higher sun and better odds.
  • Lock focus and exposure on the sun and the tower turns into a clean silhouette.

The sun is the same size in every case, about half a degree. The farther you stand, the bigger it looks next to the tower.

Accuracy

The sun position matches NOAA's solar calculator to within a few seconds, with atmospheric refraction included. The curve of the Earth is ignored, which shifts the moment by a few seconds at most. Times are in Dubai time.

Questions

Why only twice a year?
Where the sun sets slides north along the horizon from December to June and back south from June to December. It passes any one direction twice, once each way, and only on those days is it at the right height when it lines up with the tower.
Can I see it from a boat?
Yes. Many spots on the line are on the water. Switch to the boat icon and you get a point at sea.
What about sunrise?
Same idea, from spots west of the tower: Jumeirah, the coast, the sea. Tap SUNSET to switch to SUNRISE.
Does it work from my balcony?
Yes, if your balcony faces the tower from the east (for sunset) or the west (for sunrise). Open MY PLACE and set your floor. The calculation runs on your phone and your location is not sent anywhere.
